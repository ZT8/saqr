# Saqr fills the gaps UAE reporting leaves

The UAE does not lack places to report fraud. What it lacks is anything that sits *before* the report and *after* the warning. Residents have at least six official reporting entry points: the MoI platform, Dubai Police's eCrime Hub, Abu Dhabi Police's Aman, Sharjah Police's Haris system, the Public Prosecution's My Safe Society app, and 999. Yet the only public verification tool found is the Cyber Security Council's StaySafe link checker. There is no public lookup for reported phone numbers, IBANs or social media handles. There is no guided, evidence-preserving help flow for extortion victims, and no guardian feature for elderly relatives. The threat is large and current. Abu Dhabi Police alone recorded **15,642 online-fraud cases and returned Dh140 million to victims in about two years to October 2025**. The Cyber Security Council reported a **roughly 35% rise in fraudulent messages** over the year to February 2026. The State Security Department warned in January 2026 of a **spike in cyber-blackmail**. Federal Decree-Law 34/2021 already punishes these crimes heavily: online fraud carries up to AED 1M, impersonating a government entity or stealing card data up to AED 2M, and aggravated extortion up to 10 years. So Saqr's value is not new law or a new reporting endpoint. It is pre-report triage in Arabic, community intelligence, and handoff to existing police channels. The main constraints are UAE defamation and privacy law, which push Saqr to publish *indicators* such as numbers and URLs rather than accusations against named people, and to keep sensitive evidence on the victim's device. One caution for the pitch: the authorities publish **no national annual fraud total, no loss total and no age breakdown**, so several numbers a pitch might want simply do not exist publicly. The final section flags them.

## Abu Dhabi logs about 21 online-fraud cases a day while national totals stay unpublished

The most solid recent UAE figure comes from Abu Dhabi Police. Lt Col Ahmed Al Shamsi said the force "received **15,642 cases** related to online fraud" and returned **Dh140 million (about US$38m)** to victims over two years, reported in October 2025 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/10/22/abu-dhabi-police-return-38-million-to-victims-of-online-fraudsters); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/abu-dhabi-police-launch-sixth-be-cautious-campaign-to-combat-cyber-fraud-1.500317345)). That works out to about 7,800 cases a year, or **about 21 a day in one emirate**. This is our own arithmetic and should be labelled as derived. Other emirates report direction rather than counts. Dubai Police said fraud, cybercrime and economic crime fell in H1 2026 against H1 2025, and the force shut down **103 fraudulent social media accounts in Q2 2026** ([Gulf News, Aug 2026](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/dubai-sees-drop-in-fraud-and-cybercrime-cases-in-first-half-of-2026-1.500629394)). Dubai Police also said fraud reports fell across 2026, crediting the #BeAwareofFraud campaign, which involves more than 25 government and semi-government entities ([Gulf News, Oct 2026](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai/dubai-fraud-reports-fall-in-2026-as-police-step-up-scam-fight-1.500727417)). Sharjah reported "a noticeable drop" in Q1 2025, with **fake job offers and deceptive ads** still at the top of its list ([Gulf News, May 2025](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/top-online-threats-revealed-fake-jobs-fraud-ads-top-sharjahs-cybercrime-list-1.500125726)). The last clear spike on record is Sharjah's H1 2023: **351 cybercrime reports against 117 a year earlier** ([Khaleej Times/WAM, Sep 2023](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/crime/uae-over-50-increase-in-cybercrime-electronic-fraud-reports-this-year-says-top-sharjah-police-of)). The same article's e-fraud sub-figures ("11 vs 60") look transposed, and its "over 50%" headline does not match the body, so those numbers should not be quoted.

Two things are happening at once. Reported fraud is falling in 2025–2026 in the emirates that run sustained awareness campaigns, which supports the case that community-facing tools work. But exposure stays very high. GASA's survey of 1,000 residents found **seven in ten encountered a scam** in 2025, and the average resident meets one **every three days, about 111 times a year** ([GASA, Jan 2026](https://gasa.org/knowledge-base/reports/state-of-scams-in-the-united-arab-emirates-2025)). The Cyber Security Council's weekly "Cyber Pulse" said fraudulent messages rose **about 35%** over the year to 1 February 2026. It advised residents to block senders and install spam filters but named no specific reporting channel for SMS ([Khaleej Times/WAM, Feb 2026](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/cyber-security-council-warns-rise-fraudulent-messages)).

**Scam patterns.** No UAE authority publishes a percentage breakdown by scam type, so the picture comes from case descriptions. Abu Dhabi Police's October 2025 list includes:

- callers posing as government employees to get banking details;
- requests to install remote-access software;
- fake landlords or developers collecting deposits;
- fake links sent by SMS, email and social media;
- fake sales of gold, car plates, property and sheep;
- a Dh1,500 chalet deposit scam in which the victim's Emirates ID was later used to open bank accounts ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/10/22/abu-dhabi-police-return-38-million-to-victims-of-online-fraudsters)).

Courier "small delivery fee" calls and fake police requests for Emirates ID and OTP codes appear in Abu Dhabi's older (2022) case data ([Khaleej Times, Mar 2022](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/crime/uae-dh21-million-from-phone-scams-cyber-crimes-recovered-and-returned-to-victims)). On calls, Truecaller's Jan–Nov 2025 UAE data shows **banking-related calls make up 19.4% of user-reported unwanted calls**, and the financial sector almost 30% ([Zawya/Truecaller](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/companies-news/financial-sector-leads-unwanted-call-reports-in-uae-accounting-for-nearly-30-of-interruptions-jiovekbx)). The Central Bank responded structurally in May 2026. It ordered banks to move **beyond SMS OTPs**, introduced a fraud-risk data framework, and began building a central **Anti-Fraud Operations Centre** for real-time monitoring ([Gulf News, May 2026](https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/uae-banks-ordered-to-tighten-anti-fraud-security-beyond-otps-1.500548327)).

**Losses.** There is no official national loss figure. The best proxy is BioCatch's April 2026 survey of UAE bank fraud leaders ([BioCatch](https://www.biocatch.com/press-release/uae-banking-leaders-report-increasing-fraud-losses)):

- 58% said fraud losses had increased;
- 62% put their own institution's yearly losses above **AED 18.3m**;
- 95% called social media "micro-scams" a growing problem, especially for expatriates with short local banking histories;
- only 26% reimburse more than half of scam victims.

**Extortion and vulnerable groups.** Here the official signal is qualitative. The State Security Department's January 2026 warning described a spike in cyber-blackmail without giving numbers ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/uae-issues-fresh-warning-on-online-blackmail-and-threats-1.500402712)). Reported cases include a 15-year-old who blackmailed a 12-year-old girl with photos obtained through social media, and a man who used an online game to contact and blackmail children, with "hundreds of photos" found on his devices. The UAE is moving to a **minimum social media age of 15** ([Annahar/AFP, Jun 2026](https://en.annahar.com/en/323275/childhood-vs-algorithms-how-cyber-extortion-is-pushing-the-uae-to-redefine-digital-boundaries)). For older residents, the Ministry of Interior states that "the elderly are **among the most targeted groups** by cyber fraudsters". It points to their trust in official bodies and to AI voice-cloned calls that impersonate a child or relative ([Emirates 24|7, Oct 2026](https://www.emirates247.com/uae/is-someone-calling-your-elderly-parent-pretending-to-be-you-uae-experts-explain-the-ai-voice-scam-targeting-seniors/6917)).

The Cyber Security Council also says the UAE fends off **about 800,000 cyberattacks a day** ([Gulf News, Apr 2026](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/uae-faces-800000cyberattacks-daily-despite-lull-1.500513370)). That number counts attacks on infrastructure, not scams against individuals, and should not appear in the pitch as a fraud statistic.

## Decree-Law 34/2021 already prices every scam Saqr detects

Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 on Countering Rumours and Cybercrimes took effect on **2 January 2022**. It replaced the 2012 cybercrime law, and the official portal lists no amending law ([uaelegislation.gov.ae](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1526)). The law never uses the word "phishing". Phishing is caught by combining the fake-site, card-data, access-code and fraud articles. All penalties below come from the English translation of the law hosted by ADGM ([Decree-Law 34/2021, ADGM text](https://assets.adgm.com/download/assets/Federal%2BDecreeLaw%2BNo%2B34%2Bof%2B2021%2BOn%2BCountering%2BRumors%2Band%2BCybercrimes.pdf/7b3c845c864f11ef8ccdca0b2c1f6c13)). The fraud and extortion figures are independently confirmed by Abu Dhabi Judicial Department guidance ([Gulf News, 2025](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/up-to-dh1-million-fine-for-committing-electronic-fraud-in-the-uae-1.500293509)) and by a Public Prosecution alert ([Gulf News, 2022](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-dh250000-minimum-fine-possible-jail-sentence-for-electronic-blackmail-threats-1.88008714)).

| Article | Conduct (Saqr scam category) | Penalty |
|---|---|---|
| Art. 40 | Online fraud, false name or impersonation to obtain money or benefit (bank/OTP, parcel, investment, rental scams) | At least 1 year and/or AED 250,000–1,000,000 |
| Art. 11 | Fake website, email or account attributed to a person; if attributed to a **government entity** (fake police, fines, customs) | Imprisonment and/or AED 50,000–200,000; government entity: up to 5 years and AED 200,000–2,000,000 |
| Art. 15 | Capturing, cloning or using card or e-payment data without authorisation | Imprisonment and/or AED 200,000–2,000,000 |
| Art. 9 | Obtaining a PIN, password or code without permission; using it to commit a crime | AED 50,000–100,000; used for a crime: at least 6 months and/or AED 300,000–500,000 |
| Art. 42 | Extortion or threats via IT; aggravated if it threatens a crime or harm to honour or reputation (sextortion) | Up to 2 years and/or AED 250,000–500,000; aggravated: up to 10 years |
| Art. 14 | Forged electronic documents (fake fines, fake government notices) | Government document: temporary imprisonment and AED 150,000–750,000 |

The same law constrains Saqr itself. **Art. 43 (defamation, AED 250,000–500,000)** makes a public "scammer list" that names people dangerous. **Art. 44 (privacy, at least 6 months)** applies to publishing harmful material about a person "even if true", which rules out republishing victims' screenshots. **Arts. 6 and 13** penalise collecting or storing personal data without a lawful basis (AED 20,000–500,000). **Art. 18** punishes site managers who conceal or tamper with digital evidence. **Art. 53** fines platforms AED 300,000–1,000,000 for failing to comply with removal orders. The design consequences are a moderated registry of **indicators** (numbers, URLs, IBANs, handles) rather than named accusations, tamper-evident evidence logs, and a fast takedown workflow. One caveat: the research tool paraphrased the English translation, and the **Arabic text prevails**, so each penalty should be checked against the Arabic gazette before it goes on a slide.

The Personal Data Protection Law (Decree-Law 45/2021) is the other half ([PDPL text](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1972/download)):

- **Scope.** It applies if Saqr is run by a private team. It does not apply to government entities or to data held by security and judicial authorities, so a police-operated Saqr may fall outside it (Art. 2).
- **Lawful basis.** Consent is the default. The Art. 4 exceptions include **public interest** and **"judicial and security procedures"**, which are the natural basis for processing third-party scammer indicators.
- **Security.** Art. 20 requires measures such as **encryption and pseudonymisation**.
- **Breach notification.** Art. 9 requires notifying both the regulator and affected people.
- **Data protection officer.** Art. 10 requires one for large-scale processing of sensitive data. Extortion evidence (intimate, family or criminal-record data) likely meets that test.

The awkward reality is that **the PDPL Executive Regulations had still not been issued as of June 2026**. Breach deadlines, transfer rules and fines are therefore undefined ([Morgan Lewis, Jun 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/uae-establishes-federal-authority-for-artificial-intelligence-and-data)). In the meantime, DLA Piper's assessment is that Cybercrime Law Art. 13 "is likely" to apply to unlawful processing ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=AE)). A new Federal Authority for AI and Data, announced on 14 June 2026, absorbs the Data Office. It is expected, but not confirmed, to take over PDPL enforcement. For a hackathon entry, the defensible posture is to host in the UAE, collect the minimum, hash and encrypt evidence, and act as a feeder to the police rather than a database of record.

## Six reporting doors, one link checker, and no number lookup

The federal portal lists the official cybercrime channels ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/cyber-safety-and-digital-security/report-cybercrimes-online)):

- the **MoI eCrime platform** in the MOI UAE app;
- **Dubai Police's eCrime** website;
- **Abu Dhabi Police's Aman** service;
- the Federal Public Prosecution's **My Safe Society** app;
- police stations;
- **999**.

The MoI Cybercrime Platform was launched in 2024 by the Federal Department for Combating Cybercrime. It offers reporting, information submission and consultation, and refers cases to the Public Prosecution ([Gulf News, Jul 2025](https://gulfnews.com/uae/crime/uae-ministry-of-interior-launches-cybercrime-platform-5-tips-to-avoid-online-fraud-1.500215172)). Dubai's **eCrime Hub (ecrimehub.gov.ae)** is the most complete police portal. It takes reports 24/7 with evidence upload and gives tips tailored to families, seniors and children ([Emirates 24|7, Oct 2026](https://www.emirates247.com/uae-guide/dubai-police-ecrime-hub-how-to-report-cybercrime-online-recover-from-scams-and-protect-yourself/6353)). Sharjah launched its "Be Aware – Stop, Think, Protect" platform in November 2025, with interactive fraud-scenario stations and a children's station ([Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/sharjah-new-platform-cybersecurity)).

On the prevention side, the options are thin:

- **Links.** The Cyber Security Council's **StaySafe** gives a colour-coded reliability score from 11 checks. It is the only public scam-checking tool found ([Emirates 24|7, Nov 2026](https://www.emirates247.com/uae-guide/how-to-check-if-a-website-is-a-scam-in-the-uae-use-the-free-staysafe-tool-from-the-cyber-security-council/7779)).
- **Spam and telemarketing.** Since August 2024, residents have reported **92,748 numbers** to TDRA. TDRA found 3,301 violations, issued more than Dh19 million in fines and disconnected 9,433 numbers ([The National, Aug 2026](https://thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/08/06/dh19-million-in-fines-issued-and-9400-numbers-disconnected-for-telemarketing-violations)). Those reports are not published, and the 2211 channel targets telemarketing, not fraud.
- **Caller ID.** e&'s **Kashif** shows the registered name of *commercial* callers only. It says nothing about an unknown personal number ([e&](https://www.eand.ae/en/c/support/mobile/postpaid/other-services/cnap/overview-and-benefits.html)).
- **Children.** Child protection runs through the MoI's 116111 hotline and Hemayati app, and the Ministry of Education's 80085 line ([u.ae](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/children-safety)).

The pattern is clear. The official system is strong at **receiving** reports and **broadcasting** warnings, one press release at a time. It is weak at the moment of doubt, when someone holding a suspicious WhatsApp message, number or IBAN wants to know whether it has been seen before. None of the sources found mentions any of the following:

- a public lookup for numbers, IBANs or social media handles;
- a searchable feed of police alerts;
- a guided flow that helps an extortion victim *preserve* evidence before reporting;
- a feature that links an elderly parent's account to a trusted relative;
- public analytics broken down by scam type.

One caution: absence from news coverage is not proof of absence, because the official sites themselves (adpolice.gov.ae, ecrimehub.gov.ae, moi.gov.ae) were not fetched.

## Abroad, the winners close the loop from report to block

Successful foreign platforms do more than collect reports. They turn reports into blocks and publish how well that works:

- **Singapore's ScamShield** combines an app, a WhatsApp bot, the 1799 helpline and an alert channel. In Q3 2026 it issued 4.39M scam alerts and reported **86% precision and 85.8% recall** at **S$0.17 per alert**. It blacklists about 600 SMS senders a month, some within about 15 minutes. Its web-reporting pilot drew only **2.2%** of in-app volume, and **41%** of iOS registrants never returned ([OGP ScamShield updates](https://reports.open.gov.sg/scamshield/updates)).
- **Australia's National Anti-Scam Centre** recorded 481,523 reports and A$2.18bn in losses in 2025. It removed more than 7,500 scam URLs and referred 4,246 phone numbers to telcos. Older people are over-represented: **people aged 65+ are 17.1% of the population but 26.5% of losses** ([NASC, Mar 2026](https://www.nasc.gov.au/news/continued-action-critical-to-combat-fraud-as-annual-scam-losses-exceed-2-billion)).
- **India's Sanchar Saathi/Chakshu** used 770k citizen inputs to help disconnect 3.94M mobile connections ([IANS via NewKerala, Feb 2026](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/cyber-fraud-3943-lakh-mobile-connections-disconnected-227-708.htm)).
- **Commercial tools.** Google's Messages Scam Detection already supports **Arabic**, on the device, for Android SMS ([Google, Feb 2026](https://blog.google/security/staying-one-step-ahead-strengthening-androids-lead-in-scam-protection/?hl=en)). It does not see WhatsApp, and whether it is enabled in the UAE is not confirmed.
- **Intimate-image abuse.** StopNCII sets the standard: **hashing on the device** so the image never leaves the phone. It has protected 2 million images ([UK Safer Internet Centre, Nov 2025](https://saferinternet.org.uk/blog/stopncii-org-being-used-to-protect-2000000-images-online-in-the-fight-against-intimate-image-abuse)). NCMEC recorded more than 50,000 financially motivated sextortion reports in the US in 2025, up from more than 36,000 ([NCMEC, Jun 2026](https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2026/ncmec-releases-new-sextortion-data-2025)).

| **Saqr (prototype)** | Yes, explainable local engine | Yes, moderated indicators | Handoff + authority dashboard | Guided flow, on-device evidence | Guardian mode | Yes | Yes (Gulf context) | Yes |
| MoI eCrime platform | No | No | Yes (to Prosecution) | Report only | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Dubai eCrime Hub | No | No | Yes | Report only | Tips only | No | Yes | Yes |
| ADP Aman | No | No | Yes | Report only | No | Campaigns | Yes | Yes |
| CSC StaySafe | Links only | No | No | No | No | No | Not verified | Yes |
| TDRA 2211 / e& Kashif | Business caller name only | No (reports unpublished) | TDRA only | No | No | No | Partial | Yes |
| ScamShield (SG) | Yes | Reports to police | Yes, public metrics | No | No | No | Not stated | No |

The UAE rows come from the channel sources cited above. The international rows come from the sources in this section. Saqr's row describes its planned feature set, not shipped capability.

The lessons transfer directly:

- **Measure precision and recall** and publish them, as ScamShield does.
- **Meet users in WhatsApp** rather than on a website.
- **Never store intimate images.** Hash on the device and refer victims to StopNCII or Take It Down.
- **Target older users explicitly.** Losses concentrate there, and no government tool reviewed offers trusted-contact alerts.
- **Treat retention as a design problem**, given ScamShield's 41% drop-off.

## Conclusion

The pitch should not claim that the UAE lacks anti-fraud infrastructure. Judges from the police will know that it does not. The stronger argument is about where the existing system sits: it works at the *end* of the victim's journey (report, investigate, recover funds), while losses happen at the *start*. Saqr is the missing front door. It checks a suspicious item in Arabic, recognises it against community reports, and routes the user into the channels police already run. The emirates that report falling fraud in 2025–2026 are the ones running sustained community campaigns, and ScamShield's published metrics show that a prevention tool can be held to a measurable standard. Saqr should promise both: measured accuracy and a direct handoff.

The legal analysis is less a hurdle than a design specification. Publishing indicators rather than names, keeping intimate evidence on the victim's device, hosting in the UAE and logging evidence so it cannot be altered answer Arts. 43, 44, 6/13 and 18 directly. These choices also make Saqr adoptable by police: if a police force operates it, the PDPL's government exclusion simplifies compliance further. The largest open risk is the missing data. Without national case or loss totals, Saqr's authority dashboard could itself become the first systematic source of UAE scam-trend data, and that may be its most persuasive selling point to the competition's police judges.
